You are judging written responses blind. Below is a task brief, followed by
several responses written from that brief, labelled with letters. You do not
know how any of them were produced. Do not use tools.

Judge as the intended reader named or implied by the brief: someone who did
not see the session that produced the text, who has their own context and
time pressure, and who needs the meaning to arrive intact. Value clarity and
legibility, meaning that the reader takes the right meaning from the text
without effort. Do not reward brevity, simple words or short sentences for
their own sake, and do not reward length for its own sake.

For each response, list concrete problems, quoting the exact phrase:

- facts not in the brief, stated as true (invented specifics);
- guesses not marked as guesses, or hedges made stronger or weaker;
- reliance on context the reader lacks (back-references, coined labels,
  undefined terms, notation in place of sentences);
- information from the brief dropped or distorted;
- reasons lost (says what, not why, where why matters);
- precision lost (a vague plain phrase where an exact term was needed);
- performance (figures of speech or emphasis that carry no extra meaning),
  filler, or repetition;
- wrong register for the moment (too flat for a design conversation, too
  loose for documentation, too vague for an agent to act on).

Then note what each response does well that is not covered above.

Finish with a ranking from best to worst, and for each adjacent pair say how
large the gap is (slight / clear / strong) and why, in one sentence. Then
output one final line of the form:

RANKING: <letters, best first, comma-separated>
